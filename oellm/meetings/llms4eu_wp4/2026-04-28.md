## Slide 1 — Where we left off (March)
- We had just reproduced **OLMo-3-7B Instruct-SFT** as a controllable starting checkpoint and validated it with LLM-as-a-judge (Qwen3-30B-A3B) through **OpenJury**
- The plan was to begin **continued SFT on English + EU mixes** (90/80/70 % English) to fix the ~93%-English skew of the Dolci data
- Today I'll walk through what came out of that, plus a few new threads that opened up since

---

## Slide 2 — Multilingual EU SFT: what we learned
- Replaying the base SFT's own English data is the unlock — it keeps English Elo at the baseline level (~950) instead of letting it collapse to ~770
- Across 12 EU languages the sweet spot sits at roughly **75% English / 25% EU**; pushing all the way to pure-EU collapses both English *and* EU performance
- Continued SFT itself causes English forgetting — even a 100%-English continued-SFT run with no EU data at all still drops English Elo
- Cross-lingual transfer is script-dependent: Romanian (held-out, Latin script) transfers cleanly, while Greek does not
- The bottom line is that the *source* of the English replay matters more than the exact English/EU ratio

---

## Slide 3 — Dolci-Translated SFT (the headline win)
- On gemma-3-27b-it translations of Dolci into 7 EU languages, two matched-compute runs show that a **75/25 English-to-translated mix preserves English at baseline while lifting non-English Elo from 697 to 755**, and the more aggressive 25/75 mix wins on most non-English (notably Finnish, +125 over baseline) at a ~37-point English cost
- Next: quality-filter the translated pool, then run the Think-SFT analogue

## Slide 5 — Datamix-9B Instruct SFT (OpenEuroLLM 9B alpha)
- We applied the same 75/25 SFT recipe to the **OpenEuroLLM 9B alpha** (`datamix-9b-80-20`, a Llama-architecture model with a Gemma tokenizer, pretrained on 4B tokens with an 80/20 English/multilingual mix), judged with Qwen3.5-27B at 500 battles per language
- At matched context length, Datamix-9B **beats our OLMo 75/25 on most non-English languages** — most strikingly on Czech, where it sets the highest non-English Elo we've measured to date — confirming that the multilingual pretraining mix carries through into post-training; English still lags, primarily because of the alpha's 2048-token context limit

## Slide 6 — JudgeArena (NeurIPS)
- JudgeArena is our open-source framework for LLM-as-a-judge evaluation; it now unifies AlpacaEval, Arena-Hard v0.1/v2.0, MT-Bench, and **m-Arena-Hard v0.1/v2.0** (23 languages) behind a single interface, with any vLLM / llama.cpp / OpenRouter model usable as either contestant or judge and full reproducibility metadata logged for every run
- Our **tuned open-weight judges** match closed-model judges in meta-evaluations against human-preference data, both in English and multilingual settings, removing the need to depend on proprietary judges for headline numbers
- A new **simulated LMArena Elo** mode combines existing human annotations with an LLM-judge evaluation of a target model and reproduces actual LMArena Elo to within a small per-model error (leave-one-out over 20 models), giving us a low-cost stand-in for full human arena evaluation
- A **fluency-completion** mode now lets us evaluate *base* models (no chat template required) in Finnish, French, German, Spanish and Swedish — useful for the EuroLLM pretrained checkpoints
- All the numbers in this update were produced through this pipeline
